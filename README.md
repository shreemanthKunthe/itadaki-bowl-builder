# Itadaki Ramen Experience

MASTER PROMPT — ITADAKI RAMEN SHOP WEBSITE

ROLE

You are a world-class creative developer and digital art director specializing in

Awwwards-level editorial websites, restaurant branding, GSAP/ScrollTrigger,

scrollytelling and experimental typography.

Your task is to build a HIGH-FIDELITY INTERACTIVE WEBSITE PROTOTYPE for:

ITADAKI RAMEN SHOP

The goal is NOT to create a conventional restaurant website.

The goal is to translate the visual direction of an editorial Japanese food brand

into a distinctive digital experience that makes visitors:

1. WANT THE FOOD

2. WANT TO VISIT

3. KNOW HOW TO BOOK / ORDER

==================================================

1. BRAND

==================================================

Brand:

ITADAKI RAMEN SHOP

Meaning:

ITADAKI / いただき

Current brand positioning:

Japanese comfort food made accessible in India, with ramen as the hero.

Itadaki is a young, contemporary ramen brand born in India and designed to

make Japanese comfort food approachable, exciting and culturally relevant.

Existing brand line:

"A HUG FROM JAPAN."

Brand personality:

- Bold

- Young

- Warm

- Contemporary

- Playful

- Graphic

- Social

- Confident

- Slightly rebellious

- Food-obsessed

Core territory:

COMFORT, WITH ATTITUDE.

Do NOT make the brand feel like:

- a luxury Japanese restaurant

- a traditional Japanese restaurant

- an anime merchandise store

- a generic Asian restaurant

- a minimalist SaaS landing page

- a generic food delivery website

- an AI-generated restaurant template

The website should feel like an editorial piece of brand culture.

==================================================

2. CORE CREATIVE DIRECTION

==================================================

Use DIRECTION 4 as the primary visual direction.

The inspiration is an experimental editorial/art-directed website:

large typography, asymmetry, extreme scale, negative space, unexpected

image placement and strong graphic composition.

Borrow the DESIGN BEHAVIOUR, not somebody else's visual identity.

Key principles:

- Typography behaves like a graphic object.

- Images should feel art-directed rather than placed inside cards.

- Use asymmetry intentionally.

- Use extreme scale.

- Use large areas of negative space.

- Break the conventional grid occasionally.

- Create visual tension between typography and food photography.

- Keep navigation extremely simple.

- Let the food become the emotional focal point.

- Every section should feel composed like an editorial spread.

The website should feel like:

A POSTER + FOOD MAGAZINE + DIGITAL EXPERIENCE.

NOT:

A RESTAURANT TEMPLATE.

==================================================

3. COLOR SYSTEM

==================================================

Primary brand red:

#E9252F

Use red confidently but intentionally.

Supporting colors:

Cream / warm off-white:

#F7F1E6

Black:

#111111

White:

#FFFFFF

The dominant visual system should be:

CREAM + RED + BLACK + WHITE

Do not introduce:

- gradients

- purple

- blue

- neon green

- random accent colors

- excessive color variation

Red should function as both:

1. Brand identity

2. Interaction / CTA signal

==================================================

4. TYPOGRAPHY

==================================================

Typography must feel editorial and highly intentional.

Primary display:

Use a bold condensed / grotesk display typeface similar to:

Anton / Archivo Black / Barlow Condensed ExtraBold.

Supporting UI/body:

Use a clean grotesk such as:

Inter.

Japanese typography:

Noto Sans JP.

IMPORTANT:

The large ITADAKI wordmark should NOT simply look like ordinary text.

Treat "ITADAKI" as a graphic identity element.

Use oversized custom-looking geometric typography with:

- sharp diagonals

- angular cuts

- strong geometric forms

- dramatic scale

- irregular visual rhythm

If a custom SVG/graphic wordmark is practical, use it.

Never use more than 2–3 typefaces across the experience.

Typography hierarchy must be extreme.

==================================================

5. HERO — THE MAIN EXPERIENCE

==================================================

The hero is the most important part of the website.

CORE IDEA:

"IT STARTS WITH A BOWL."

The visitor should BUILD A RAMEN BOWL THROUGH SCROLL.

This is NOT just a video.

The scroll position controls the progression.

Hero sequence:

FRAME 01 — EMPTY

Show a clean empty ramen bowl.

Huge red ITADAKI typography behind / around the bowl.

Copy:

IT STARTS

WITH A BOWL.

Supporting copy:

Japanese comfort food, made for the kind of craving

that doesn't need a reason.

Small interaction prompt:

SCROLL TO BUILD ↓

FRAME 02 — BROTH

As the user scrolls:

Broth appears inside the bowl.

Small editorial label:

01 / BROTH

Optional microcopy:

RICH. DEEP. WARM.

FRAME 03 — NOODLES

Noodles enter the bowl.

Label:

02 / NOODLES

Microcopy:

THE FOUNDATION.

FRAME 04 — PROTEIN / TOPPINGS

Ingredients progressively appear.

For example:

- chicken

- egg

- mushrooms

- spring onion

- nori

- other relevant toppings

Labels should feel like editorial annotations,

NOT cooking instructions.

FRAME 05 — COMPLETE

The bowl becomes the finished ramen.

The composition settles.

Typography resolves.

Show:

ITADAKI.

A HUG FROM JAPAN.

CTA:

EXPLORE THE MENU →

Secondary CTA:

RESERVE A SEAT

==================================================

6. HERO ANIMATION BEHAVIOUR

==================================================

Use GSAP ScrollTrigger.

The hero should be pinned during the bowl-building sequence.

The scroll controls the animation timeline.

Do NOT make the bowl randomly fly around the screen.

The bowl should remain relatively stable and centered.

The CONTENT of the bowl changes progressively.

The animation should feel:

- smooth

- premium

- tactile

- deliberate

- cinematic

Avoid:

- excessive parallax

- bouncing

- spinning

- cheesy particle effects

- random rotations

- excessive easing

- gimmicky transitions

The visitor should feel like they are physically constructing the bowl

through their scroll.

After the bowl is complete, transition naturally into the editorial homepage.

==================================================

7. IMPORTANT IMAGE REQUIREMENT

==================================================

Use the PROVIDED RAMEN BOWL IMAGE as the exact visual reference for the bowl.

Do not redesign the bowl.

Do not change:

- bowl shape

- food appearance

- ingredients

- proportions

- photography style

For the prototype, create the bowl-building sequence using layered assets,

image sequences, masks or composited elements.

If individual ingredient assets are unavailable,

create a visually convincing prototype using placeholder layers while

preserving the final bowl's appearance.

The final frame MUST visually match the provided bowl reference as closely

as possible.

==================================================

8. TRANSITION AFTER HERO

==================================================

When the bowl is completed, do NOT immediately jump into a conventional

restaurant section.

Break the hero composition into an editorial layout.

The giant ITADAKI typography can shift,

crop,

move off-screen,

or transform into the next section.

The transition should feel like:

EMPTY → BUILD → COMPLETE → EXPLODE INTO THE ITADAKI WORLD

==================================================

9. TOP PICKS

Create an editorial "Top Picks" section.

Heading:

THE ONES WORTH SLURPING.

Supporting copy:

Our go-to bowls, bites and sips.

Made for first-timers, regulars,

and anyone who suddenly finds themselves craving ramen.

Instead use an asymmetrical editorial composition.

Mix:

- large food photography

- smaller supporting images

- red blocks

- black typography

- cream space

- tiny labels

- oversized numbers

Potential dishes:

Yasai Shoyu Ramen

Tori Miso Ramen

==================================================

Create a section that explains the brand without becoming a corporate

"About Us" section.

Headline direction:

NOT JUST A BOWL.

Copy direction:

Come alone.

Bring your people.

==================================================

Create a visually dominant food section.

==================================================

Use subtle interactions throughout:

- image masking

- scroll-linked movement

- hover image scale

- subtle cursor interactions

- editorial text reveals

- section transitions

- slight parallax

But remember:

LESS, BUT BETTER.

Every animation needs a purpose.

==================================================

17. DESIGN RULES

==================================================

STRICTLY AVOID:

❌ Generic restaurant template

❌ Rounded card grids

❌ Glassmorphism

❌ Gradients

❌ Excessive shadows

❌ Floating blobs

❌ Random Japanese illustrations

❌ Excessive Japanese symbols

❌ Anime clichés

❌ Generic stock imagery

❌ Excessive animations

❌ Giant generic CTA buttons everywhere

❌ Cookie-cutter responsive layouts

❌ Excessive border-radius

❌ "AI website" aesthetic

DO:

✓ Extreme typography

✓ Editorial composition

✓ Strong negative space

✓ Asymmetry

✓ Food photography

✓ Red / cream / black palette

✓ Strong visual hierarchy

✓ Scroll storytelling

✓ Intentional whitespace

✓ Controlled motion

✓ Strong conversion moments

==================================================

18. RESPONSIVE

==================================================

Desktop is the primary art-directed experience.

Mobile must preserve the concept rather than simply shrinking the desktop.

On mobile:

- keep the bowl central

- maintain the scroll-building interaction

- reduce typography scale intelligently

- simplify compositions

- preserve the red/cream identity

- keep CTA accessible

- maintain readability

Do not create a completely different visual identity for mobile.

==================================================

19. TECHNICAL

==================================================

Preferred stack:

Next.js

React

GSAP

ScrollTrigger

Lenis or equivalent smooth scrolling

Use reusable components.

Keep animations performant.

Use lazy loading for large imagery.

Use proper semantic HTML.

Use accessible buttons and navigation.

Do not sacrifice performance for visual effects.

==================================================

20. MOST IMPORTANT CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

==================================================

DO NOT DESIGN A WEBSITE THAT LOOKS COOL.

DESIGN A WEBSITE THAT MAKES ITADAKI FEEL DESIRABLE.

The visitor journey should be:

WHO ARE THEY?

↓

THAT LOOKS GOOD.

↓

I WANT THAT.

↓

THIS PLACE HAS A VIBE.

↓

WHERE IS IT?

↓

LET'S GO.

The website's emotional objective:

MAKE ME CRAVE THE BOWL.

The website's commercial objective:

MAKE ME VISIT ITADAKI.
